Part I — The Technology Landscape

Quantum computing uses quantum mechanical phenomena — superposition, entanglement, interference — to perform computations that classical computers cannot efficiently simulate. The core advantage: certain problem classes (optimisation, quantum chemistry simulation, cryptography) scale exponentially better on quantum hardware than on classical silicon.

The key technical challenge: quantum decoherence. Quantum states are fragile; interactions with the environment cause errors at rates that currently limit circuit depth. The path to fault-tolerant quantum computing requires quantum error correction — encoding logical qubits across many physical qubits such that errors can be detected and corrected. Google's Willow result (December 2024) demonstrated for the first time that error rates decrease as more qubits are added — the inflection point that validates the fault-tolerant path.

Part III — The IONQ vs D-Wave Distinction

IONQ ≠ D-Wave. Never conflate these technologies. They represent fundamentally different approaches to quantum computing with different use cases, time horizons, and competitive moats.

IONQ (trapped ion, IONQ): Universal gate-based quantum computing. Every algorithm that runs on a classical computer can theoretically run on IONQ hardware given sufficient qubit count and fidelity. IONQ's 2026 hardware achieves algorithmic qubit fidelity metrics that make it the leading pure-play quantum company for near-term gate-based quantum advantage. Key customers: US government, financial services (quantum machine learning).

D-Wave (QBTS, quantum annealing): Specialised optimisation hardware. D-Wave does NOT implement universal quantum gates. It solves combinatorial optimisation problems (scheduling, logistics, materials discovery) by finding energy minima in a quantum annealing landscape. D-Wave has a 5,000+ qubit machine available NOW via cloud — but it only solves a specific class of problems. Advantage Leap cloud platform has paying customers today.

Part IV — The 2032 Horizon: What Changes

By 2032, the BL base case (70% probability) is that fault-tolerant quantum computers with 1,000+ logical qubits will demonstrate quantum advantage in: (1) quantum chemistry simulation for drug discovery; (2) financial portfolio optimisation at hedge fund scale; (3) materials science (battery, superconductor design); and (4) cryptographic applications.

The investment implication: the 2032 quantum advantage cohort will be worth $1–10 trillion in incremental economic value. The current market caps of pure-play quantum companies are $1–5 billion — pricing in a small fraction of the optionality. This makes quantum the highest-optionality allocation in the Phase 2 RELOAD portfolio at 5% weighting.

Critical risk: quantum computing timelines have slipped historically. The 5% weighting reflects this — it is an asymmetric bet, not a core holding. Size for the moon-shot, not the balance sheet.
